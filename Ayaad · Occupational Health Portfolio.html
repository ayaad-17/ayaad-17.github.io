Lifting injury claims review

Problem

Workers’ compensation claims from the adult Emergency Department were coded with “lifting” as the cause of injury. Before deciding on prevention steps, the department needed to know what actually caused those injuries.

What I did

  • Built a claims tracker covering claim type, status, cause, body part, and department for each lifting-coded claim from 2024 to 2026.
  • Read each claim narrative and wrote a short summary of how the injury actually happened.
  • Noted where the narrative did not match the cause code, and where equipment came up as a factor.

What it showed

Only about half of the claims described lifting or transferring a patient. Three involved repositioning a patient who was already in bed, and one involved no lifting at all. That points to a coding question as well as a safety one, and one narrative pointed to a specific equipment need (a stretcher for car-to-stretcher transfers).

Fresenius contractor oversight

Problem

Fresenius dialysis staff work inside five JHM hospitals, but they are employed and onboarded by Fresenius. Occupational Health was getting questions about who owned which records, who to call at each site, and which contracted staff were actually compliant.

What I did

  • Collected points of contact for each site from a long email chain and confirmed the division of work: JHM OH handles baseline and annual fit testing and seasonal flu vaccine, and Fresenius keeps everything else.
  • Took a responsibility flowchart through three drafts to a final version that color-codes JHM and Fresenius steps and shows the quarterly audit cycle.
  • Built a directory and compliance brief listing every contracted employee by site, with status on the OSHA questionnaire, fit testing, and flu records. It includes site-level compliance rates and charts.

Result

One reference document that answers “who do I call” and “is this person cleared” for each site. It also sets expectations for monthly roster sharing and how fast document requests get answered.

Occupational Health lobby display

Problem

Employees passing the Occupational Health office at Suburban had no quick way to find hours and contact details, see when their department’s fit testing was due, or get reminders about safety requirements.

What I did

  • Designed a looping slide set for the department’s TV screen that pairs practical information with safety messaging.
  • Turned the 2025 annual fit-testing schedule into a one-slide view organized by department.
  • Condensed the High Reliability universal skills and the eye protection policy into slides someone can read while walking past.
  • Added a recognition slide to celebrate staff.

Result

A ready-to-run TV loop, dated July 1, 2026. I used the same visual style for related materials: N95 and eye protection tip sheets, flu clinic schedules, and multi-site compliance charts.

Flu Champions coverage plan

Problem

Flu Champions are employees who volunteer to give vaccines at flu clinics. Their availability came in as scattered dates and times for three hospitals, and no one could see whether each site actually had coverage.

What I did

  • Compiled every submission into one workbook with a tab for each site: Suburban, Howard County, and Sibley.
  • Built a summary tab that counts filled slots per site and flags any site at zero as a gap, using formulas so it updates as new availability comes in.
  • Put “any site” volunteers on a separate tab so they could be assigned where the need was greatest.

Result

The gaps were visible right away. Suburban was well covered, Howard County was thin, and Sibley had no volunteers. That gave the team a clear place to send flexible volunteers and focus recruitment.

Mass fit-testing clinic workflow

Problem

Mass N95 fit-testing clinics bring in large numbers of employees in a short window. Each one goes through check-in, three questionnaires, an RN review, possible medical referral, the fit test itself, and documentation in Cority. Handoffs and guest-login records were where things got missed.

What I did

  • Mapped the process from two sides: the employee’s path (QR check-in, SSO or guest login, the OH authorization, OSHA, and TB questionnaires) and the OH staff path.
  • Separated the medical history and no-medical-history routes. Employees who cannot complete a fit test are routed to a PAPR or an NP referral.
  • Wrote out the Cority documentation steps, including the extra steps for guest logins that were easy to skip.
  • Revised both diagrams through three versions.

Result

Two diagrams the team can use to train clinic staff and to check that each employee’s record is complete before they leave.

Master competency tracker

Problem

OH staff competencies were spread across separate source forms, each with its own rating scale and renewal rules. These included fit testing validation, vaccine preparation and administration, and point-of-care urine drug screening. No single view showed who was current and who was overdue.

What I did

  • Pulled every checklist item from the source forms into one item bank. For each item I recorded its domain, rating scale, assessment method, and whether the form states a renewal period.
  • Built an employee tracker with dates, results, and status for each competency area, with dropdowns to keep entries consistent.
  • Wrote a roll-up formula that gives each person one overall status.

Result

One workbook where a manager can see at a glance who needs attention. The item bank also shows where the source forms leave renewal requirements unstated.
